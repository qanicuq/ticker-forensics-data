## How a stock gets on this list

Must pass all of these (SKILL.md section 3): listed on NYSE, Nasdaq or Cboe; market cap under $2B; average volume over 50K shares a day; no loss in any of the last 5 years; current ratio of at least 1.5; long-term debt plus leases no more than 110% of net current assets; price no more than 15 times 3-year average earnings.

Then ranked with equal weight on four things: cheapest on 3-year average earnings, cheapest against tangible book, cash flow best backing up earnings, and most liquid assets for the price.

Graham tests = the 7 enterprising-investor tests in SKILL.md. Red flags are quick forensic checks (SKILL.md section 4); a flag means look harder, not automatically avoid.

Near 52-wk low? = price within 10% of its lowest price in the past year (Dreman and Neff hunt among recent losers). The % is how far above that low it is. n/a = listed under a year.

Neff ratio = (yearly growth of total earnings over the last 5 annual reports % + dividend yield %) ÷ P/E on the latest year. Higher is better; Neff wanted about twice the market's ratio. It uses total earnings, not per-share earnings, so buybacks or new shares can make it a little off.
